# Launch post templates

Replace the `{...}` parts. Always attach a 15-second screen recording of the app doing its main thing.

## Launch post (Sky Social, X, Bluesky, Threads)

    Built a {app name} in 25 minutes with Python.

    {one-line what it does}. Free and open source.

    Download: {release link}
    Code: {repo link}

    Next up: {next app}.

## Build-in-public thread

    1/ I'm shipping one small Python app at a time and open-sourcing all of them. This week: {app name}.
    2/ The idea: {problem it solves in one sentence}.
    3/ How it works: {2 to 3 sentences, one screenshot or the recording}.
    4/ The hard part: {one real bug or decision}. Fix: {what you did}.
    5/ Download for Windows, macOS or Linux: {link}. It's unsigned, so on Windows click "More info > Run anyway".
    6/ Need a version for your business? I take custom builds: {contact link}.

## LinkedIn

    It's free and open source. If your team needs something similar built to fit your workflow, I take on custom projects: {contact link}.

    {release link}

## Reddit

Rules differ by subreddit. Read each one before posting; many ban self-promotion or want a specific format and flair. Lead with the problem or the code lesson, not the link. Good fits: the language or hobby subreddit the app serves, and "show your project" threads where they exist.

## 5-day cadence

| Day | Post |
|---|---|
| 1 | Teaser: the problem and a 5-second clip |
| 2 | Launch post with the download link |
| 3 | Behind the scenes: one code snippet or bug story |
| 4 | Answer feedback, show a change you made because of it |
| 5 | Recap numbers (downloads, stars) and announce next app |

## Recording tips

- 15 seconds, one feature, no intro.
- Dark window on a clean desktop; zoom the app to fill the frame.
- Put the download link in the first reply or the bio, not only in the caption.
